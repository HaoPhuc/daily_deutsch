## Theory

### A1-G02 Personal pronouns and *sein*
*sein* (to be) is irregular: learn its forms by heart. It's the most common verb in German.

| Person | Pronoun | *sein* | English |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1st sg | ich | **bin** | I am |
| 2nd sg (informal) | du | **bist** | you are |
| 3rd sg | er / sie / es | **ist** | he / she / it is |
| 1st pl | wir | **sind** | we are |
| 2nd pl (informal) | ihr | **seid** | you (all) are |
| 3rd pl | sie | **sind** | they are |
| formal (sg + pl) | Sie | **sind** | you are |

**Examples**
- *Ich bin Phuc. Ich bin Student.* (I am Phuc. I am a student.)
- *Du bist Lukas, oder?* (You're Lukas, right?)
- *Frau Weber ist nett.* (Frau Weber is nice.)
- *Wir sind in Eindhoven.* (We are in Eindhoven.)
- *Sind Sie Herr Lang?* (Are you Mr Lang?)

**Pitfalls**
1. **sie / sie / Sie:** *sie ist* = she is; *sie sind* = they are; *Sie sind* = you are (formal, always capital S). The verb form and the capital letter tell you which one.
2. **No article for jobs:** *Ich bin Student.* not ~~Ich bin ein Student.~~ Women add *-in*: *Sie ist Studentin.*
3. **ihr seid, not ~~ihr sind~~:** *ihr* is the only plural with its own form.

a) Who is *sie* in sentence 3?
b) Who is *sie* in sentence 6?
c) Who is *wir*?
d) Is Jonas using *du* or *Sie* with the reader? Which word shows it?
